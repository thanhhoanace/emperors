# The periphery around 200 CE, and one advantage per emperor

Research notes for the "all of China" map expansion (lon 73°–135°E, lat 18°–54°N) in *Tam Quốc Loạn Nhập*.
Written 2026-09-25. Background only: nothing in the repo was changed.

**How this was gathered.** WebFetch was blocked by the egress proxy for Wikipedia, Britannica and chinaknowledge.de, so each fact comes from a web search result summary. The page the summary relied on is given as the source link. Most are English Wikipedia; a few are Baidu Baike (English), Fandom or travel sites, and those are marked *(weak)*.
- `[?]` marks a claim I could not confirm with a search. It is from memory or commonly repeated, so check it before it goes into `docs/`.
- Numbers from ancient histories (army sizes, "300,000 people") are the chroniclers' numbers and are often inflated. Use them for **relative** scale only.
- Names are given as English / Hán-Việt (the UI language), with Chinese where useful.

## 0. Short answer

- **Qin Shi Huang and Han Wudi come from *before* 200 CE**, so they do not bring future technology. Their edge is **lost frontier knowledge and founder's infrastructure**. By 200 CE the Eastern Han had abandoned or lost what they built: the Ordos (Hetao), the Hexi corridor, the Western Regions protectorate, Hainan, the Lingqu route to Lingnan, and the road to Yunnan. They know where these are, how they were taken and how to supply them.
- **Tang Taizong and Hongwu come from *after* 200 CE**, so they bring real later knowledge:
  - Tang Taizong: paired stirrups and heavy cavalry, the Sui Grand Canal route, a steppe-diplomacy "Heavenly Khagan" model, and knowing that Tibet will rise.
  - Hongwu: gunpowder weapons, Champa (early-ripening) rice, fired-brick city walls, the Ming Yunnan–Guizhou post road, and the weisuo military-farm system.
- **The periphery in 200 CE is a ring of weak, divided or abandoned lands** around a Central Plain packed with Han armies. That fits the owner's premise: the emperors win the ring and the warlords hold the core.
  - Northern frontier commanderies: abandoned (Cao Cao formally dissolved six of them in 215).
  - Hexi: cut off (made a separate Yong province in 194).
  - Western Regions: no Han official after 175.
  - Liaodong: a separatist Gongsun regime.
  - Lingnan and Jiaozhi: the Shi family's private domain.
  - Nanzhong: local magnates and tribes.
  - Southeast hills: unconquered Shanyue.
- **Suggested starts:** Liu Che to **Hexi (Vũ Uy)**; Li Shimin stays at **Tấn Dương** with the steppe opened north of him; Qin Shi Huang stays in **Quan Trung**, linked to the empty **Ordos** by his Straight Road; Zhu Yuanzhang moves to **Poyang/Dự Chương**. The alternatives are given in §C.5.

## A. The periphery in 200 CE, region by region

Each block gives who held the region and how strong they were, the resources, the chokepoints, and a game note.
Coordinates are approximate [lon, lat] for a possible province seat.

### A1. Northeast: Liaodong, Wuhuan, Goguryeo, Buyeo, Yilou

**Gongsun regime in Liaodong (Liêu Đông, Công Tôn Độ → Công Tôn Khang).** Seat Xiangping (Tương Bình, Liaoyang) [123.17, 41.27].
- Gongsun Du, Han-appointed Administrator of Liaodong, turned it into a private state. He held Liaodong and Xuantu (Huyền Thố) and extended to Lelang (Lạc Lãng).
- He sent a **naval expedition across the Bohai** to occupy Donglai on the Shandong peninsula and named an inspector of his own new "Yingzhou" province. He styled himself Marquis of Liaodong.
- In **204** his son Gongsun Kang inherited Liaodong, Xuantu and Lelang. He split southern Lelang into **Daifang** (Đới Phương), invaded Goguryeo (a 204 succession feud, then again in **209**), and pushed the Goguryeo capital to the Yalu (Hwando).
- The regime lasted until Sima Yi destroyed it in 238.
- *Game:* a neutral minor power with a fleet, and a sea edge Liaodong ↔ Qing (Donglai).
- Sources: [Gongsun Du](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gongsun_Du), [Gongsun Kang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gongsun_Kang), [Sansang of Goguryeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sansang_of_Goguryeo), [Sima Yi's Liaodong campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sima_Yi%27s_Liaodong_campaign)

**Wuhuan (Ô Hoàn) of Liaoxi, Youbeiping and Liaodong; Tadun (Đạp Đốn).** Base Liucheng (Liễu Thành, Chaoyang) [120.45, 41.57].
- Allied to the Yuan family. After Yuan Shao died, the Yuan brothers joined them, and Tadun's combined Wuhuan and Han following was *said* to number up to 300,000 (people, not soldiers).
- In **207** Cao Cao followed Tian Chou's advice and took a **disused road through the abandoned Former-Han frontier** (the Lulong pass / Xifengkou route) rather than the coastal road. He surprised them at **White Wolf Mountain** (Bạch Lang Sơn, near Lingyuan [119.4, 41.2]).
- Afterwards he resettled the Wuhuan south, held families hostage, and used Wuhuan men as elite cavalry.
- Tian Chou himself had a refuge settlement on **Xuwu mountain** (Từ Vô sơn) that grew to **5,000+ households**.
- *Game:* this is the model "hidden pass" story. Map knowledge of an old road lets an army bypass a chokepoint.
- Sources: [Battle of White Wolf Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_White_Wolf_Mountain), [Wuhuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuhuan), [Cao Chun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cao_Chun), [Tian Chou *(Baike, weak)*](https://baike.baidu.com/en/item/Tian%20Chou/948094)

**Goguryeo (Cao Câu Ly).** Capital near Ji'an, Jilin [126.18, 41.12]; moved to Hwando in 209 under Gongsun pressure. It was hostile to Liaodong. Cao Wei (Guanqiu Jian) sacked Hwando in 244.
- Source: [Sansang of Goguryeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sansang_of_Goguryeo)

**Buyeo (Phù Dư)** [~126.5, 43.8] and **Yilou (Ấp Lâu, the old Sushen country)**:
- Buyeo is described in the *Sanguozhi* as 2,000 *li* across with **80,000 households**. It bordered Goguryeo to the south, Yilou to the east and the Xianbei to the west.
- Yilou lived in forested mountains more than 1,000 *li* north-east of Buyeo.
- *Game:* Manchuria's interior is forest, marsh and mountain, reachable only through Liaodong. Treat Yilou and the far north-east as **nearly impassable wilderness**.
- Sources: [Buyeo *(NWE)*](https://www.newworldencyclopedia.org/entry/Buyeo_(state)), [Yilou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yilou)

**Chokepoints.**
- **Liaoxi corridor.** The only easy land road between North China and the North-east is the narrow coastal plain between the Yan hills and the Bohai. The Ming built **Shanhai Pass (Sơn Hải quan)** at its narrowest point only in **1381** (Xu Da), so in 200 CE no fortress stood there.
- **Liao marshes (Liêu Trạch).** Mud "200 *li* deep", impassable to men and horses. Tang Taizong's army in 645 had to lay an earth causeway to cross.
- Sources: [Shanhai Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanhai_Pass), [Goguryeo–Tang War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goguryeo%E2%80%93Tang_War) (and the NamuWiki summary in search results)

### A2. Northern steppe: Xianbei, Southern Xiongnu, the abandoned Ordos

**Xianbei (Tiên Ti).**
- Tanshihuai's confederation broke up after his death in **181**. South of the Gobi it split three ways:
  - **Budugen (Bộ Độ Căn)**, with "tens of thousands", around **Yunzhong and Yanmen** (Hohhot–Datong) [111.6, 40.8].
  - **Kebineng (Kha Bỉ Năng)** across **Dai and Shanggu** (Zhangjiakou / Bashang) [115, 41.5]. His was the strongest group.
  - Minor eastern chiefs beyond **Liaoxi, Youbeiping and Yuyang**.
- Kebineng rose after 207. **Han refugees taught him weapon-making and writing**, and he came to command **100,000+ mounted archers**. He absorbed Budugen in 233 and was assassinated on Wei orders in **235**.
- *Game:* in 200 CE the Xianbei are divided, so steppe diplomacy can pick them off one by one. They are also the largest cavalry reservoir on the map.
- Sources: [Kebineng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kebineng), [Budugen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budugen), [Tanshihuai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanshihuai), [Xianbei *(Baike)*](https://baike.baidu.com/en/item/Xianbei/1495479)

**Southern Xiongnu (Nam Hung Nô).**
- In **189** the chanyu Yufuluo (Ư Phu La) settled with his followers at **Pingyang** (Bình Dương, Linfen) [111.52, 36.08], inside Bing province. His brother Huchuquan (Hô Trù Tuyền) succeeded him in 195.
- In **216** Cao Cao kept Huchuquan at Ye and split the people into **Five Divisions** around Taiyuan, each under Han supervisors.
- *Game:* in 200 CE a Xiongnu horde sits **inside** Bing province, next to Li Shimin's current start.
- Sources: [Huchuquan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huchuquan), [Qubei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qubei), [Han-Zhao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Zhao_Kingdom)

**The abandoned Ordos and Hetao (Hà Sáo, "Tân Tần Trung") and the Yinshan line.**
- The Han northern commanderies had emptied as people fled the nomads. In **215** Cao Cao formally abandoned **Dingxiang, Yunzhong, Wuyuan, Shuofang, Shang and Beidi**. Yunzhong and Dingxiang shrank to counties under a new Xinxing commandery.
- So in 200 CE the great Yellow River loop is **effectively no-man's land**, roamed by Xianbei and Xiongnu.
- Beidi, which in 140 CE had only 18,637 people, shows how empty it already was.
- Sources: [Dingxiang Commandery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dingxiang_Commandery), [Yunzhong Commandery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yunzhong_Commandery), [Beidi Commandery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beidi_Commandery), [Bing province *(Substack)*](https://deniseemerson.substack.com/p/the-province-of-bingzhouintroduction)

**Mongolian plateau (Mạc Bắc).**
- The Northern Xiongnu were broken at the Yanran mountains in **89 CE** (200,000 of 81 tribes surrendered) and pushed west.
- By 200 CE the plateau was Xianbei country [?: the remaining Xiongnu "calling themselves Xianbei" comes from the *Hou Hanshu* and was not confirmed].
- Crossing the Gobi is the costliest move on the map. Han Wudi's **119 BCE** Mobei campaign needed 100,000 cavalry, over 100,000 infantry, and **140,000 supply horses**.
- *Game:* treat Outer Mongolia as off-map (outside present-day China). Keep Inner Mongolia as steppe provinces.
- Sources: [Dou Xian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dou_Xian), [Battle of Mobei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Mobei)

**Northern passes.**
- **Yanmen** and **Juyong**:
  - Both are ancient gates.
  - Juyong's Ming fortress dates from **1368** (Xu Da, Chang Yuchun).
  - I did not find a detailed source for Yanmen [?].
- Ming knowledge of where the steppe must be held: Juyong (1368) and Shanhai Pass (1381) are Hongwu's. The full "Nine Garrisons" line (Liaodong–Jizhou–Xuanfu–Datong–…–Gansu) was completed after his reign.
- Sources: [Nine Garrisons of the Ming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nine_Garrisons_of_the_Ming_dynasty), [Nine Great Passes *(Baike)*](https://baike.baidu.com/en/item/Nine%20Great%20Passes/1460478)

### A3. Northwest: Liangzhou warlords, Qiang, Di, Hexi, Dunhuang

**Ma Teng (Mã Đằng) and Han Sui (Hàn Toại).**
- Both were products of the **184 Liang Province rebellion**, in which the Qiang, the **Lesser Yuezhi auxiliaries of Huangzhong** and local Han rose together. It lost Liang province to the Han court.
- In the early Jian'an years they fought each other. **Zhong Yao and Wei Duan reconciled them in 200.**
- In **211**, at Tong Pass, Ma Chao and Han Sui's coalition of **Han, Qiang and Hu** troops was defeated by Cao Cao.
- Their bases were Longxi/Tianshui and Jincheng (Kim Thành, Lanzhou [103.8, 36.06]).
- Sources: [Ma Teng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ma_Teng), [Ma Chao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ma_Chao), [Battle of Tong Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Tong_Pass), [Liang Province rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liang_Province_rebellion)

**Song Jian (Tống Kiến)** declared himself "King of the River Sources Who Pacifies Han" at Fuhan (Linxia) [103.2, 35.6]. He stayed independent for **30+ years**, until Xiahou Yuan crushed him in 214. This is a real micro-state, which makes it a good neutral.
- Source: [Song Jian *(Kongming's Archives)*](https://kongming.net/encyclopedia/Song-Jian)

**Qiang (Khương).**
- Semi-nomadic herders of Gansu and the Qinghai–Tibet plateau, in a century of wars with the Han.
- Duan Jiong's 167–169 campaign alone claims 180 battles, 38,000 Qiang killed and **427,000 head of cattle, horses, sheep, mules and camels** captured, at a cost of 4.4 billion cash.
- *Game:* Qiang provinces are **horse and livestock** sources. They are cheap to win by diplomacy and hard to pacify by force.
- Huangzhong (Hoàng Trung, Xining [101.78, 36.62]) held the Qiang and the Lesser Yuezhi (Tiểu Nguyệt Thị).
- Sources: [Qiang (historical people)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qiang_(historical_people)), [Yuezhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuezhi), [Huangzhong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huangzhong,_Xining)

**Di (Đê) of Wudu (Vũ Đô)** [105.0, 33.4]. Yang Qianwan (Dương Thiên Vạn) was "King of the Baiqing Di" on Mount Chouchi, one of four Di powers in Wudu. In 211 they sided with Ma Chao, Han Sui and Zhang Lu against Cao Cao. Wudu is a mountain buffer between Longxi, Hanzhong and Shu.
- Sources: [Chouchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chouchi), [Di (Five Barbarians)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Di_(Five_Barbarians))

**Hexi corridor (Hà Tây tứ quận).**
- Wuwei (Vũ Uy/Cô Tang [102.64, 37.93]), Zhangye (Trương Dịch [100.45, 38.93]), Jiuquan (Tửu Tuyền [98.5, 39.73]) and Dunhuang (Đôn Hoàng [94.66, 40.14]).
- Created by Han Wudi after Huo Qubing expelled the Xiongnu in **121 BCE** (King Hunye surrendered).
- In **194** these commanderies were too far from, and cut off from, the Liang provincial seat. The court split them off as **Yong province with its seat at Guzang (Wuwei)**.
- In 200 CE they are an isolated, leaderless string of oases. Local strongmen, Qiang and Xianbei press in, and there is no big warlord. [?: the details of who governed there in 200 are thin in the sources I could reach.]
- Sources: [Hexi Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hexi_Corridor), [Yongzhou (ancient China)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yongzhou_(ancient_China)), [Four Commanderies of Hexi *(Baike)*](https://baike.baidu.com/en/item/Four%20Commanderies%20of%20Hexi/1451077)

**Dunhuang and the gates of the west.**
- **Yumen Pass (Ngọc Môn quan)**, ~90 km NW of Dunhuang [93.86, 40.35], was built by Han Wudi soon after 121 BCE. It is named for the **jade caravans**.
- **Yang Pass (Dương Quan)**, ~70 km SW [94.07, 39.93], dates from about 120 BCE.
- The two sit 68 km apart at either end of the Han wall's Dunhuang extension.
- Later, Cang Ci (Administrator of Dunhuang c. 227–230s) ran fair-price trade with western merchants. Earlier (c. 215–218) Dunhuang's elites had to name an acting head themselves.
- [?] The *Sanguozhi* says Dunhuang had "no administrator for 20 years" before Cang Ci.
- Sources: [Yumen Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yumen_Pass), [Yang Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yang_Pass), [Cang Ci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cang_Ci), [Ma Ai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ma_Ai)

### A4. Far west: Western Regions (Tây Vực), Wusun, Kushan

- **The Han presence had lapsed.** The Chief Official of the Western Regions (Tây Vực trưởng sử) is last recorded in **175**.
  - Ban Yong had held the office in 123 with **500 freed convicts at Liuzhong** (Turpan basin).
  - Cao Wei restored it later, and the office lasted to about 328.
  - In **222** the kings of **Shanshan, Kucha and Khotan** each sent tribute to Wei.
  - In **229** Vasudeva of the Kushans (the "Greater Yuezhi") sent an envoy.
  - So in 200 CE the oases are **independent city-states waiting for a patron**.
- **Oasis kingdoms:**
  - Shanshan (Thiện Thiện), with Loulan as a dependency, at Lop Nur [~88–90, 39–40.5].
  - Gaochang/Jushi (Cao Xương/Xa Sư, Turpan [89.53, 42.85]).
  - Kucha (Quy Tư [82.96, 41.72]).
  - Khotan (Vu Điền [79.92, 37.11]).
  - Shule (Sơ Lặc, Kashgar [75.99, 39.47]).
  - Some sources claim the Kushans controlled Kashgar, Yarkand and Khotan after a 2nd-century campaign. This is debated.
- **Khotan jade.** Nephrite from the White Jade and Black Jade rivers of Khotan went via the Tarim routes, the Hexi corridor and the Jade Gate to Chang'an. This "Jade Road" is older than the Silk Road.
- **Wusun (Ô Tôn), Ili valley** [~81.3, 43.9]. The Wusun had driven the Yuezhi out of the Ili. Han Wudi married Princess Xijun to their king for **1,000 horses**, and first named Wusun horses "heavenly horses". This part of Xinjiang is inside present-day China.
- **Routes and hazards:**
  - A northern route runs along the Tianshan foot (Turpan–Kucha–Aksu) and a southern route along the Kunlun foot (Shanshan–Khotan–Yarkand).
  - The **Taklamakan interior is impassable**.
  - Northern Xiongnu remnants raided Hami and Turpan through the early 2nd century.
- *Game:* the far west is a **trade-and-horse** reservoir with small garrisons. Ferghana (Đại Uyển) is outside the map (about 71°E), so model it as an off-map node reached from Kashgar.
- Sources: [Protectorate of the Western Regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protectorate_of_the_Western_Regions), [Chief Official of the Western Regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Official_of_the_Western_Regions), [Ban Yong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ban_Yong), [Shanshan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanshan), [Kushan Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kushan_Empire), [Vasudeva I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasudeva_I), [Kanishka's Central Asian campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanishka%27s_Central_Asian_campaign), [Weilüe: Peoples of the West](https://depts.washington.edu/silkroad/texts/weilue/weilue.html), [Kingdom of Khotan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Khotan), [Jade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jade), [Wusun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wusun), [Iranica: Xiongnu](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/xiongnu/)

### A5. Tibetan plateau and Qinghai

- There was no Tibetan state in 200 CE. The plateau held "numerous small kingdoms and tribes": Qiang groups in the east (Fa Qiang, the Yak Qiang etc.) and **Zhangzhung** (Tượng Hùng) in the west (Ngari, c. 500 BCE–625 CE).
- A **Tangmao** polity near Lhasa, founded by a Qiang branch fleeing the Han in 101 CE, is claimed. The source is weak.
- *Game:* the plateau is **nearly impassable** (altitude and supply) and should be ocean-like for movement. The Qinghai lake basin / Huangzhong is its only usable edge.
  - For Tang Taizong it matters as knowledge: Tibet unifies in the 7th century, and the route Chang'an–Qinghai–Lhasa (the **Tang–Tubo road**) opens in 641.
- Sources: [Zhangzhung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhangzhung), [Qiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qiang_(historical_people)), [Tangmao/Sumpa *(tourism site, weak)*](https://tibetantrekking.com/sumpa-kingdom/)

### A6. Southwest: Hanzhong, Shu roads, Nanzhong (Yunnan–Guizhou)

**Zhang Lu's Hanzhong (Hán Trung)** [107.03, 33.07].
- A Five Pecks of Rice Daoist theocracy from **191**, independent for 30 years; Zhang Lu surrendered to Cao Cao in 215.
- **Yangping Pass (Dương Bình quan)** [106.6, 33.15] is its gate, called "Yi province's throat".
- Sources: [Zhang Lu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhang_Lu_(Han_dynasty)), [Battle of Yangping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Yangping), [Way of the Five Pecks of Rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Way_of_the_Five_Pecks_of_Rice)

**The Shu roads (Thục đạo).**
- North section, across the Qinling: the Chencang, Baoxie, Tangluo, Ziwu and Qishan roads.
- South section: the **Jinniu/Stone Cattle road** (built by Qin in 316 BCE to conquer Shu), the Micang road and the Lizhi road.
- **Jianmen/Jiange (Kiếm Môn/Kiếm Các)** [105.56, 32.2] is the key gate on the southern leg.
- *Game:* Guanzhong ↔ Hanzhong ↔ Shu edges are **only** these roads, each a narrow edge with strong defence. Qin (the Stone Cattle road) and Han Wudi know them.
- Sources: [Shudao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shudao), [Stone Cattle Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_Cattle_Road), [Jianmen Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jianmen_Pass), [Qinling Shu Roads intro](https://qinshuroads.org/docs/html/Shu_Roads_Introduction.htm)

**Three Gorges.** Kuimen and Baidicheng (Bạch Đế thành) [109.57, 31.04] are the river gate between Shu and Jing.
- Sources: [Baidicheng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baidicheng), [Qutang Gorge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qutang_Gorge)

**Nanzhong (Nam Trung).**
- Han commanderies existed only in name. Power sat with **local great clans** (Yong Kai 雍闓 of Yizhou commandery, the Cuan, Meng and others) and tribes.
- In 225 Yong Kai rebelled and brought in **Meng Huo (Mạnh Hoạch)**, a local leader.
  - The "seven captures" are only a bare line in the histories; Romance of the Three Kingdoms invented the details and characters such as Lady Zhurong and King Mulu.
  - After the campaign Shu took **gold, silver, cinnabar and lacquer, plus plough oxen and war horses**.
- **Yongchang (Vĩnh Xương)** [99.5, 25.5] under Lü Kai stayed loyal.
  - It was created in **69 CE** when the **Ailao (Ai Lao)** king submitted: 77 settlements, **50,000 households, over 550,000 people**.
  - It sat on the "Shu–Shendu" (Southwest Silk) Road towards Burma and India.
- **Dian (Điền)**, the bronze-casting kingdom around Dian Lake (Kunming [102.7, 25.0]), was annexed by Han Wudi in **109 BCE**. The gold "King of Dian" seal was found in 1956.
- **Yelang (Dạ Lang)** in Guizhou became Zangke (Tang Kha) commandery in **111 BCE**. The Zangke river leads down to Nanyue.
- **Hazard, "miasma" (chướng khí).** In Ma Yuan's Jiaozhi campaign (42–44 CE) **40–50% of the soldiers died** of *zhang* epidemics. Northern armies in the tropical south should lose troops to disease, especially in summer.
- Sources: [Zhuge Liang's Southern Campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhuge_Liang's_Southern_Campaign), [Meng Huo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meng_Huo), [Yongchang Commandery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yongchang_Commandery), [Ailao Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ailao_Kingdom), [Dian kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dian_kingdom), [Han conquest of Dian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_conquest_of_Dian), [Yelang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yelang), [Ma Yuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ma_Yuan_(Han_dynasty)), [UW *Good Formulas* ch. 4 on zhangqi](https://uw.manifoldapp.org/read/badf3f68-a71c-4bff-96d1-b026443d0469/section/0d05865a-41ee-4dd1-a017-600542eb3aea)

**Wuling Man (Vũ Lăng Man / Ngũ Khê Man)** of western Hunan [Changde, 111.7, 29.0]. Their chieftain **Shamoke (Sa Ma Kha)** joined Liu Bei at Xiaoting in 221–222. They form a hill-tribe buffer between Jing and Guizhou.
- Sources: [Shamoke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shamoke), [Battle of Xiaoting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Xiaoting)

### A7. South and southeast: Lingnan, Jiaozhi, Hainan, Shanyue, Fujian, Taiwan

**Shi Xie (Sĩ Nhiếp) and the Shi family's Jiao province.**
- Shi Xie was Administrator of **Jiaozhi (Giao Chỉ)** from **187 to 226**, with his seat at Luy Lâu (N. Vietnam, outside present-day China).
- His brothers held **Hepu (Shi Yi), Jiuzhen (Shi Wei) and Nanhai (Shi Wu, seat Panyu/Guangzhou)**. Cangwu and Rinan were also under the clan's sway.
- Buddhism, brought by sea from India, was the most common religion of Jiaozhi by about 200.
- He submitted to Sun Quan's inspector Bu Zhi only around **210**.
- **Correction to the current data:** in 200 CE Lingnan was *not* Sun Quan's. `jiao` (Phiên Ngung) should be neutral, held by the Shi.
- Sources: [Shi Xie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shi_Xie), [Jiaozhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiaozhi), [Shi Xie's SGZ bio (tr.)](https://kongming.net/novel/sgz/shixie.php)

**Maritime trade.**
- **Hepu (Hợp Phố)** [109.2, 21.66] and Xuwen were the Han's ports for the maritime Silk Road and **pearl markets**. Ships sailed down the Vietnamese coast to the Malay peninsula and on to India.
- The "Roman" embassy of **166** arrived through Rinan with ivory, rhino horn and tortoiseshell.
- Sources: [Hepu tombs *(Antiquity)*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/abs/hepu-han-tombs-and-the-maritime-silk-road-of-the-han-dynasty/E25B5D92D22A110D3CFB5AD74F89F38A), [Daqin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daqin)

**Hainan (Châu Nhai / Đạm Nhĩ).**
- Han Wudi created the Zhuya and Dan'er commanderies in 110 BCE. After native revolts, Jia Juanzhi's memorial had Zhuya **abolished in 46 BCE**, leaving a single county under Hepu.
- Sun Quan re-took it only in **242** with 30,000 men.
- In 200 CE it is **abandoned native land** (Li people).
- Sources: [Jia Juanzhi memorial (fanhan.org)](https://fanhan.org/chapter-2/jia-juanzhi/), [Hainan *(Baike)*](https://baike.baidu.com/en/item/Hainan%20Province/987241)

**Taiwan (Di Châu 夷洲).** In 230 Sun Quan sent Wei Wen and Zhuge Zhi with **10,000 men**. They found Yizhou, captured several thousand people, and lost **80–90% of the fleet to disease**. This is a cautionary event for any overseas venture.
- Sources: [VOA fact check](https://www.voanews.com/a/fact-check-so-taiwan-has-been-chinese-for-two-millennia-lets-check-the-history/6743451.html), [Wei Wen *(Kongming)*](https://kongming.net/encyclopedia/Wei-Wen)

**Shanyue (Sơn Việt).**
- Upland Yue tribes in the hills of southern Anhui, Zhejiang, Jiangxi and Fujian. Wu's campaigns kept failing because they retreated into the hills.
- In **234** Zhuge Ke blockaded Danyang, seized the harvest, and forced a surrender that yielded **40,000 conscripts**.
- *Game:* a hill-tribe neutral that bleeds attackers but pays out manpower once starved or besieged.
- Sources: [Shanyue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanyue), [Zhuge Ke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhuge_Ke)

**Fujian (Mân).**
- Minyue was annexed in **110 BCE** and much of its people moved to the Jianghuai area; Dong'ou was moved out entirely.
- In 200 CE Fujian is thinly held by Sun's man **He Qi** (Commandant of Southern Kuaiji since 196). The Jian'an commandery (9 counties) came later.
- The coastal plain around Houguan (Fuzhou [119.3, 26.08]) is cut off by the Wuyi mountains.
- Sources: [He Qi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/He_Qi), [Minyue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minyue), [Siege of Dong'ou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Dong%27ou)

**Lingnan passes, the "Five Ridges" (Ngũ Lĩnh / Nam Lĩnh).** Five crossings of the Nanling: Yuecheng, Dupang, Mengzhu, Qitian and **Dayu/Meiling (Đại Dữu/Mai Lĩnh)**.
- **Lingqu canal (Linh Cừ)** at Xing'an [110.67, 25.6] joins the north-flowing Xiang to the south-flowing Li. It was built in **214 BCE** by Shi Lu with 100,000 soldiers to supply Qin's southern war, and it is the only boat route between the Yangtze and Pearl river systems.
- **Mei Pass road (Mai Lĩnh)** [114.3, 25.25]: in 200 CE this is a mountain track. The wide paved road was cut only in **716** by Zhang Jiuling (Tang), and it later became the main north–south artery.
- Sources: [Lingqu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingqu), [Nanling Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanling_Mountains), [Mei Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mei_Pass), [Meiguan Ancient Road *(Baike)*](https://baike.baidu.com/en/item/Meiguan%20Ancient%20Road/1450461), [Qin campaign against the Baiyue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qin_campaign_against_the_Baiyue)

## B. What each emperor knows that 200 CE does not

### B1. Qin Shi Huang (Tần Thủy Hoàng, reigned 247/221–210 BCE)

He is older than the Han. His edge is **founding infrastructure and conquest routes that the Eastern Han let decay**, plus the Qin state machine.

1. **Irrigation that made Qin rich.**
   - **Zhengguo Canal (Trịnh Quốc cừ, 246 BCE)** irrigated **40,000 *qing*** of saline Guanzhong land. It "made Qin rich and powerful".
   - **Dujiangyan (Đô Giang Yển, 256 BCE)** was Li Bing's Qin project that made the Chengdu plain the "Land of Abundance".
   - With the Lingqu, these are the three great pre-Han water works. Qin knows how to build, restore and run them.
   - Sources: [Zhengguo Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhengguo_Canal), [Dujiangyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dujiangyan)
2. **The Straight Road (Trực Đạo, 212 BCE) and the imperial highways (Trì Đạo).**
   - Meng Tian built **700+ km in two years with 300,000 men**, running straight north from near Xianyang to **Jiuyuan (Baotou)**. It was a leveled military highway for rapid troop moves.
   - The **chidao** network radiated from Xianyang into Yan, Qi, Longxi and Ba-Shu.
   - Sources: [Qin Straight Road *(Baike)*](https://baike.baidu.com/en/item/Qin%20Straight%20Road/1450595), [Meng Tian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meng_Tian), [Chi Dao and Zhi Dao *(Shaanxi gov)*](https://en.shaanxi.gov.cn/as/hac/ch/201704/t20170427_1595074.html)
3. **Taking the Ordos, and the Great Wall.**
   - In **215 BCE** Meng Tian drove the Xiongnu out of the Ordos with **300,000**.
   - The wall linked the Qin, Zhao and Yan walls from **Lintao to Liaodong**, 5,000+ km.
   - In 200 CE that same Ordos is abandoned land (A2).
   - Sources: [Qin campaign against the Xiongnu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qin_campaign_against_the_Xiongnu), [Qin wall *(ChinaHighlights)*](https://www.chinahighlights.com/greatwall/history/qin-dynasty-wall.htm)
4. **Conquering the south.**
   - The **Lingqu (214 BCE)** was built because the Qin army was stuck at Yuecheng mountain with no supply.
   - The **500,000-man, five-column** Baiyue war was costly: commander Tu Sui was killed. Qin learned the hard way that Lingnan is won by **supply lines, not numbers**.
   - Zhao Tuo, a Qin general, later ruled the south from Panyu.
   - The **Five-Foot Way (Ngũ Xích Đạo)** was pushed from Sichuan to Qujing in east Yunnan.
   - Sources: [Lingqu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingqu), [Qin campaign against the Baiyue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qin_campaign_against_the_Baiyue), [Zhao Tuo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhao_Tuo), [Yunnan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yunnan)
5. **Standardisation.** Script, weights and measures, coinage, **axle width** (carts fit the same ruts: logistics), law and the commandery system. Qin crossbow triggers were mass-produced to interchangeable tolerances.
   - Sources: [Qin reforms *(Fiveable, weak)*](https://fiveable.me/the-archaeology-of-ancient-china/unit-5/qin-dynasty-standardization-reforms/study-guide/S1Bx5NUcLkd2eVWH), [Mass production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_production)
6. **The Stone Cattle Road**, used by Qin in **316 BCE** to conquer Shu. Qin knows the northern gate into Sichuan.
   - Source: [Stone Cattle Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_Cattle_Road)

### B2. Han Wudi, Liu Che (Hán Vũ Đế, reigned 141–87 BCE)

He is the architect of the Han frontier that the Eastern Han has since lost. He knows **every route out of China that the Han ever opened**.

1. **Hexi and the Silk Road gates.**
   - Huo Qubing's **121 BCE** campaign led to the Four Commanderies of Hexi (Wuwei, Zhangye, Jiuquan, Dunhuang).
   - The **Yumen and Yang passes** were founded around 121–120 BCE.
   - Zhang Qian's missions opened the Western Regions.
   - Sources: [Hexi Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hexi_Corridor), [Huo Qubing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huo_Qubing), [Yumen Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yumen_Pass), [Zhang Qian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhang_Qian)
2. **Heavenly horses.**
   - First the Wusun: Princess Xijun was traded for **1,000 horses**, and theirs were first called "heavenly horses".
   - Then Ferghana, in the **War of the Heavenly Horses (104–101 BCE)**. Li Guangli won about 30 top horses and **3,000 others**, and Dayuan became a client.
   - Han state herds grew from about 300,000 to **450,000+** under Wudi.
   - Sources: [War of the Heavenly Horses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Heavenly_Horses), [Ferghana horse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferghana_horse), [Li Guangli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li_Guangli), [Atlantis Press: horse in Han](https://www.atlantis-press.com/article/125983022.pdf)
3. **Tuntian (military farming) on the frontier.**
   - The first military agro-colonies were Wudi's: **Luntai and Quli** in the Tarim, with several hundred soldier-farmers each, and watchtowers from Dunhuang to Lop Nur. They made distant garrisons **self-feeding**.
   - Sources: [Tuntian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuntian), [tuntian *(chinaknowledge)*](http://www.chinaknowledge.de/History/Terms/tuntian.html)
4. **The salt and iron monopoly (117 BCE)** and nationalised coinage, used to fund war. The *Discourses on Salt and Iron* record the debate about it.
   - The cost of his wars was an almost empty treasury, which is already modelled as `upkeep: 1.3`.
   - Sources: [Discourses on Salt and Iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discourses_on_Salt_and_Iron), [Han–Xiongnu wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han%E2%80%93Xiongnu_wars)
5. **The deep-steppe strike, Mobei 119 BCE.** Two columns of 50,000 cavalry with **140,000 supply horses**. This is logistics knowledge for crossing the Gobi.
   - Source: [Battle of Mobei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Mobei)
6. **Southern and south-western routes.**
   - Tang Meng learned in Panyu that Shu sauce came down the **Zangke river**. That route was used in the **111 BCE** conquest of Nanyue, alongside land and sea columns (Lu Bode, Yang Pu).
   - **Kunming Lake** was dug in Chang'an in **120 BCE** to train a navy for the Dian lake region. Dian was annexed in **109 BCE**, and Yelang/Zangke in **111 BCE**.
   - Minyue and Dong'ou were annexed in **110 BCE**. Hainan got commanderies in **110 BCE**.
   - Sources: [Han conquest of Nanyue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_conquest_of_Nanyue), [Kunming Lake *(Baike)*](https://baike.baidu.com/en/item/Kunming%20Lake/22532), [Han conquest of Dian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_conquest_of_Dian), [Han campaigns against Minyue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_campaigns_against_Minyue)
7. **Gojoseon / Lelang (108 BCE).** A land-and-sea invasion across the Bohai created the four Korean commanderies, with Lelang lasting to 313. He knows the sea route to Liaodong and Korea.
   - Sources: [Han conquest of Gojoseon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_conquest_of_Gojoseon), [Four Commanderies of Han](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Commanderies_of_Han)
8. **Farming technology.** The **Longshou canal**, China's first shaft-and-tunnel (qanat-like) canal; the Baiqu canal (95 BCE); Zhao Guo's **daitian** alternating-field method.
   - Sources: [Longshou canal (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/374012605_Research_on_the_Structure_and_Construction_Technology_of_Longshou_Canal_Engineering_in_the_Western_Han_Dynasty), [Han economy *(chinaknowledge)*](http://chinaknowledge.org/History/Han/han-econ.html)

### B3. Tang Taizong, Li Shimin (Đường Thái Tông, reigned 626–649)

He brings **four centuries of later knowledge**, above all about the steppe, cavalry and the far west.

1. **Breaking a steppe empire and ruling nomads through diplomacy.**
   - In the **629–630** campaign Li Jing surprised Illig Qaghan with **3,000 cavalry** at Yinshan (near Hohhot), and the Eastern Turkic Khaganate fell.
   - Taizong was hailed **"Heavenly Khagan" (Thiên Khả Hãn)** by the nomads.
   - In **646** he destroyed Xueyantuo. The Tiele and Uyghurs submitted and got prefectures under the Yanran/Anbei protectorate, with a road of **68 relay stations** to Chang'an.
   - Nomads were ruled through **jimi (ki mi, "loose-rein") prefectures**, and Turkic and Tiele cavalry were used en masse: **100,000 Tiele cavalry** at Kucha in 648. Generals included Ashina She'er and Qibi Heli.
   - Sources: [Tang campaign against the Eastern Turks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_campaign_against_the_Eastern_Turks), [Battle of Yinshan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Yinshan), [Khan of Heaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khan_of_Heaven), [Taizong vs Xueyantuo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Taizong%27s_campaign_against_Xueyantuo), [Protectorate General to Pacify the North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protectorate_General_to_Pacify_the_North), [Jimi system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimi_system), [Turks in the Tang military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turks_in_the_Tang_military)
2. **Stirrups and heavy cavalry.**
   - In 200 CE there are **no paired riding stirrups**. The earliest single mounting stirrup is from 302, the earliest full pair from **322**, and they were widespread by 477, probably through Xianbei influence.
   - Li Shimin's own guard was the **Xuanjia (Huyền Giáp) black-armoured cavalry**, 1,000–3,500 strong.
   - At **Hulao (621)** he waited until Dou Jiande's army was exhausted, then broke it with a cavalry charge, winning two rivals in one battle.
   - Sources: [Stirrup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stirrup), [Jin and Sixteen Kingdoms military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_history_of_the_Jin_dynasty_and_the_Sixteen_Kingdoms), [Battle of Hulao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Hulao), [Xuanjia *(Dragon's Armory, weak)*](http://dragonsarmory.blogspot.com/2018/03/unit-tang-elite-vanguards-jet-black.html)
3. **Fubing (phủ binh) and horse administration.**
   - About **634** militia units (later called Zhechongfu), each 800–1,200 men. The soldiers farmed equal-field land and self-equipped, so the state paid little.
   - State herds reached about **700,000 horses** by the mid-7th century, on the **Longyou and Hexi pastures**.
   - Sources: [Fubing system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fubing_system), [Horses in ancient and imperial China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horses_in_ancient_and_Imperial_China), [Tang horse ranches *(Cambridge)*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/eurasian-empires-in-antiquity-and-the-early-middle-ages/tang-chinas-horse-power-the-borderland-breeding-ranch-system/055D2AA5B89727A7D24E8D4963B64CFF)
4. **Western Regions and Qinghai.**
   - **Tuyuhun (634–635)**: Li Jing and Hou Junji marched for five months and won north-east of Qinghai Lake.
   - **Gaochang (640)** was annexed and the **Anxi protectorate** set up at Jiaohe.
   - **Karasahr (644, 648)** and **Kucha (648)** followed.
   - Sources: [Taizong vs Tuyuhun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Taizong%27s_campaign_against_Tuyuhun), [Taizong vs the Western Regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Taizong%27s_campaign_against_the_Western_Regions), [Protectorate General to Pacify the West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protectorate_General_to_Pacify_the_West)
5. **Tibet.**
   - He knows the plateau will unify. The Tibetan attack on **Songzhou (Songpan) in 638–639**, on the north-west edge of Sichuan, was repulsed.
   - **Princess Wencheng** went to Songtsen Gampo in **641** along the Chang'an–Qinghai–Lhasa route (later the Tang–Tubo road).
   - Sources: [Tibetan attack on Songzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_attack_on_Songzhou), [Princess Wencheng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_Wencheng), [Tangbo road *(tourism, weak)*](https://www.tibettrip.com/arts-culture/ancient-tangbo-road.htm)
6. **The Sui Grand Canal (605–610).**
   - Centred on Luoyang, 2,400 km from Zhuojun (Beijing) to Hangzhou: the Yongji, Tongji and Jiangnan canals. It was built to supply the Goguryeo wars, at a horrific human cost.
   - He knows exactly where a Yellow River–Huai–Yangtze link works.
   - Sources: [Sui dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sui_dynasty), [Grand Canal *(chinaknowledge)*](http://www.chinaknowledge.de/History/Terms/yunhe.html), [Timeline of the Sui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_the_Sui_dynasty)
7. **Liaodong lessons (645).**
   - He crossed the Liao marshes on an earth causeway and took Yodong and Baegam.
   - He then failed at **Ansi**, a garrison said to be about 4,000, against an army of 200,000, and retreated at winter.
   - This is knowledge of the northeast's winter and supply limits.
   - Sources: [Battle of Ansi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Ansi), [Goguryeo–Tang War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goguryeo%E2%80%93Tang_War)

### B4. Hongwu, Zhu Yuanzhang (Minh Thái Tổ, reigned 1368–1398)

He brings **1,150 years of later knowledge**. His edge is river warfare, gunpowder, southern agriculture and the logistics of settling soldiers.

1. **Yangtze and lake naval warfare.**
   - **Lake Poyang (30 Aug–4 Oct 1363):** about **200,000 vs 650,000**. He used smaller, lighter ships in squadrons and **fire ships** against Chen Youliang's tower ships. Chen was killed by an arrow while breaking out of the blockade.
   - Nanchang held for **85 days** under Zhu Wenzheng.
   - He rose as a river power: he crossed the Yangtze in **1355** and took Nanjing (Jiqing) in **1356**.
   - Sources: [Battle of Lake Poyang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Lake_Poyang), [Zhu Wenzheng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhu_Wenzheng), [Chen Youliang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chen_Youliang), [USNI: River pirate to Ming emperor](https://www.usni.org/magazines/naval-history-magazine/2010/june/river-pirate-ming-emperor)
2. **Gunpowder weapons.**
   - By **1380** the Ming army had about **130,000 gunners** in a force of 1.3–1.8 M. Doctrine aimed for about 10% gunners.
   - The oldest dated Ming bronze gun is from **1377**. Jiao Yu ran the arsenal. Mu Ying was ordered to make **thousands of hand cannons** for the 1386–88 Mong Mao war.
   - In 200 CE gunpowder does not exist.
   - Sources: [Gunpowder weapons in the Ming dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunpowder_weapons_in_the_Ming_dynasty), [Huochong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huochong), [Huolongjing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huolongjing)
3. **Weisuo (vệ sở) military households and tuntian.**
   - A garrison (wei) is **5,600** men, a battalion 1,120. Soldiers farm in peace.
   - The **border ratio is 3/10 guard, 7/10 farm**; the interior ratio is 2/10 guard, 8/10 farm.
   - He planted garrison settlements (tunbao) along the new **Hunan–Guizhou–Yunnan post road** after conquering Yunnan.
   - Sources: [Weisuo *(Britannica)*](https://www.britannica.com/topic/weisuo), [Ming military *(chinaknowledge)*](http://www.chinaknowledge.de/History/Ming/ming-military.html), [Tuntian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuntian), [Tunbao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunbao), [Tunpu settlements, central Guizhou](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/13467581.2026.2653287)
4. **Brick walls and grain first.**
   - Zhu Sheng's advice of **1357**: "build high walls, store grain widely, be slow to proclaim yourself king" (cao trúc tường, quảng tích lương, hoãn xưng vương).
   - The **Nanjing wall (1366–1386)** is 33+ km of **fired brick**, 350 M bricks, 14–20 m high, and took 200,000 workers.
   - Han walls in 200 CE are **rammed earth**, which is exactly what the project's `hancity.js` renders. So a brick upgrade would also *show* on the map.
   - Source: [City Wall of Nanjing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Wall_of_Nanjing)
5. **Southern agriculture.**
   - **Champa rice** was brought in from Fujian by Song Zhenzong in **1012**: drought-resistant, ripening in under 100 days, day-length-insensitive. It made **double-cropping** possible and opened hill land to rice.
   - By Song–Ming "when Su and Hu ripen, the realm is fed" (Tô Hồ thục, thiên hạ túc). The empire's granary had moved to the lower Yangtze and the Tai, Poyang and Dongting lake basins.
   - Sources: [Champa rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Champa_rice), [Champa rice in Song *(Rice journal)*](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12284-011-9079-6), [Jiangnan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiangnan)
6. **The whole map, including the southwest.**
   - The **Ming conquest of Yunnan (1381–82)** used 250,000–300,000 men under Fu Youde, Lan Yu and Mu Ying. Disease losses were heavy.
   - The **Xiang–Qian–Dian post road** through central Guizhou followed. Guizhou became a province in 1413, after his reign, but the road is his.
   - **Buir Lake (1388):** Lan Yu with 150,000 destroyed the Northern Yuan horde. That is steppe experience.
   - Sources: [Ming conquest of Yunnan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_conquest_of_Yunnan), [Yunnan under Ming rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yunnan_under_Ming_rule), [Battle of Buir Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Buir_Lake)
7. **Northern defence line.** Juyong (1368) and Shanhai Pass (1381) were built in his reign, so he knows which northern gates matter. The Nine Garrisons system was completed after him.
   - Sources: [Shanhai Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanhai_Pass), [Nine Garrisons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nine_Garrisons_of_the_Ming_dynasty)
8. **Registers.**
   - **Yellow Registers and the lijia system (1381)**: 110 households per *li*.
   - **Fish-scale land registers (1387)**: a cadastral survey that exposed hidden landholdings. That means more tax from the same land.
   - Sources: [Yellow Registers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_Registers), [Fish-Scale Registers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish-Scale_Registers)
9. **Horses from Tibet for tea.** The Ming **tea-horse offices (Trà mã ti)** on the Shaanxi and Sichuan frontier traded tea for Tibetan and Central Asian horses. This covers the Ming horse shortage without the steppe.
   - Sources: [Tea Horse Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tea_Horse_Road), [Ming–Tibet relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming%E2%80%93Tibet_relations)
10. **Later canal and sea routes** (Yuan-era knowledge he inherits):
    - the Yuan **Huitong canal (1289)**, the link that let the Grand Canal run north without the Luoyang detour;
    - the **sea grain route from 1282**, Liujiagang (by Suzhou) around Shandong to Zhigu (Tianjin).
    - Sources: [caoyun *(chinaknowledge)*](http://www.chinaknowledge.de/History/Terms/caoyun.html), [History of the Port of Tianjin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Port_of_Tianjin)

## C. Candidate unique advantages (mechanics in plain language)

### C0. Engine hooks these ideas assume

These are small, data-driven additions. Every number below is only an example; it must be measured with `npm run sim`, per the AGENTS.md lessons.

- **Province tags:** `resources: ["horses","iron","salt","jade","silk","copper","rice","trade"]`, `hazard: "miasma"|"desert"|"plateau"|"marsh"`, and `frontier: true` for periphery provinces.
- **Neighbour edges with attributes:** `{to, kind:"pass"|"river"|"sea"|"road"|"desert", name:"Đồng Quan", defense:1.3, hidden:true, knownBy:["qin_shihuang"]}`.
  - A hidden edge exists only for the faction that knows it (White Wolf 207 is the model).
  - A sea edge needs a fleet or a coastal province.
- **Projects (công trình):** a multi-turn variant of 🏛 Nội chính on a named site that unlocks a permanent effect, for example a new edge or a fertility bonus. They are cheap to show on screen: a label, progress bar and scaffolding on the 3D map.
- **Hazard attrition:** an army entering a miasma, desert or plateau province loses a fraction of the committed troops unless it is native or has the right knowledge.
- **Nomad and tribe neutrals:** neutral provinces with cavalry garrisons. Diplomacy (🕊) with them could produce a **levy** (troops join the winner) instead of only annexing land.
